Giant Illuminated Dragonflies for the Austin Creek Show

Mechanical Engineering InternWaterloo Greenway ConservancyMay 2022 – Jan 2023Austin, TX

Every fall, Austin's Creek Show installs large-scale illuminated art over Waller Creek. I designed and led fabrication of four metal dragonfly sculptures with 14-foot-plus wingspans — taking them from artist sketches to engineered structures that survived wind, floods, and a month of public display.

From artist sketches to CAD

Working from client and artist drawings, I designed four unique CAD assemblies in SolidWorks through extensive iteration and prototyping — each dragonfly with its own wing geometry, mast, and creek-bed mounting structure.

Fabrication: lasers, welds, and LEDs

I led full-scale fabrication: eight unique wing designs laser-cut via Fusion 360 CAM, 200+ welded joints, soldered wiring for four LED display systems, and water-resistant electronics packaging that kept everything lit above a live creek.

Engineering for wind and floods

A sculpture standing in a creek must survive flash floods. Through FEA in SolidWorks Simulation and ANSYS plus dimensional analysis, I reduced peak current-induced forces on the creek structures by 30% — significantly improving high-wind and flood resilience.

The final show

All four dragonflies flew for the full run of Creek Show, lighting up the creek for thousands of visitors every night.
